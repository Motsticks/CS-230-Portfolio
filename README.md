# CS 230 Portfolio Reflection: The Gaming Room

## Client and Software Requirements

The client for this project was The Gaming Room, which wanted to expand its game, Draw It or Lose It, into a web-based application that could support multiple platforms. The software needed to support multiple teams and players, maintain unique identifiers for game objects, and ensure that only one instance of the game existed. The design also considered operating systems, memory management, storage, and the overall architecture needed to build a reliable application.

## What I Did Well

I did particularly well at organizing the software design document into sections that explained the client's requirements and the technical decisions involved in developing the application. I compared different operating platforms and considered the advantages and disadvantages of each. This helped me communicate my recommendations clearly to the client and development team.

## What I Found Helpful About the Design Process

Working through the design document helped me understand the importance of planning before writing code. Identifying requirements, defining classes, and considering how different components interact gave me a clearer direction for development. This process can help prevent mistakes and reduce the amount of rework needed later.

## What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve the technical explanations of my architectural recommendations. I would provide more specific examples of how the selected operating platform, memory management, and data storage would work together. I would also include additional diagrams where they would help developers understand the design more easily.

## Interpreting and Implementing User Needs

I interpreted the client's needs by focusing on creating a multiplayer game that could support multiple teams and players while maintaining consistent game information. I considered unique identifiers, relationships between game objects, and the ability to expand the application in the future. Considering user needs is important because software should solve the client's actual problems, provide a good user experience, and meet functional and technical requirements.

## My Software Design Approach

My approach was to identify the client's requirements, compare possible solutions, and organize the application into components with specific responsibilities. I used object-oriented design concepts to consider classes, objects, and their relationships. In future projects, I would continue using requirements analysis, UML diagrams, platform comparisons, and iterative testing. I would also consider security, scalability, and maintainability early in the process so that the final application is easier to develop and support.
